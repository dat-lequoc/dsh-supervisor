The brain: persona, protocol, and the .agi/ contract

A supervisor is mostly prose. This chapter writes the persona that turns your preset into the Main Agent — the goal ceremony, the question protocol, the file discipline — and tests the ceremony end to end with the native goal tool.

You are about to replace text with roughly a page of protocol. Two writing rules first, because persona text is engineering, not vibes:

  • Don’t restate what infrastructure guarantees. The settle notice arrives whether or not the persona mentions it; the child’s report tool exists regardless. The persona’s job is the decisions: what to do when those things happen.

The native goal tool (what “record the goal” means)

The persona says “record the goal with the goal tool.” That tool comes from the tool-goal row your preset inherited; the goal service and its session driver live host-side (the base bundle mounts them — packages/bundle/base/cordis.patch.yml, rows goal/tool-goal), and the web UI renders the current goal for the session. That display is your Progress-tab replacement for the goal itself; progress.jsonl covers the feed. Zero UI code.

Test it live — milestone 2 acceptance: the goal ceremony

Drive this script as the user, in a fresh main-agent session:

  1. Give a vague mission: “I want a tidy, well-documented summary of the DSH docs folder. Make it happen.”
  2. Expect an interview, not action. It should ask clarifying questions (where should output go? what depth? what’s out of scope?) — not spawn anything yet.
  3. Answer tersely; let it iterate. Expect: it writes /root/agi-lab/.agi/GOAL.md and asks for explicit confirmation.
  4. Say “confirmed.” Expect: it records the goal via the goal tool (the goal appears in the session’s UI), and appends a line to progress.jsonl.
  5. Amendment discipline: say “actually, also cover the cookbook subfolder.” Expect: an entry in GOAL_AMENDMENTS.md after your confirmation — GOAL.md itself untouched (stat -c %Y .agi/GOAL.md before/after to prove it).
  6. Config discipline: say “set questionWaitMinutes to 2 for me.” Expect: it refuses to edit config.json and instead proposes the change (and logs a config-proposal). You edit the file yourself.

Not yet expected to work: the patience timer (needs chapter 8’s schedule tools) and spawning (chapter 9). If it tries to schedule_create and fails with “unknown tool” — good, that is exactly the gap the next chapter fills.

Why the interview is worth being strict about

Everything downstream — task decomposition, worker prompts, milestone checks — inherits its quality from GOAL.md. The ceremony is also your one deliberately blocking interaction, so it is where the human puts in the minutes that save the machine hours. If the agent under-asks, tighten the CEREMONY paragraph (e.g. add “ask at least about output location, success criteria, and out-of-scope”) and re-test; persona iteration is normal and cheap.

What exists now

A supervisor that can lock a goal, keep honest files, and refuse to touch what isn’t its own. It still cannot wake itself on a timer (chapter 8) or delegate (chapter 9).
