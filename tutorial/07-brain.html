The brain: persona, protocol, and the .agi/ contract

A supervisor is mostly prose. This chapter writes the persona that turns your preset into the Main Agent — non-blocking goal setup, the question protocol, the file discipline — and tests the supervisor's workspace-grounded frontend over the native goal domain.

You are about to replace text with roughly a page of protocol. Two writing rules first, because persona text is engineering, not vibes:

  • Don’t restate what infrastructure guarantees. The settle notice arrives whether or not the persona mentions it; the child’s report tool exists regardless. The persona’s job is the decisions: what to do when those things happen.

The non-blocking frontend over the native goal domain

The native goal service, projection, and session driver remain host-side. The preset disables only the inferred-intent @deepseek-ai/dsh-tool-goal frontend and mounts dsh-supervisor/goal. Its start_goal writes a canonical GOAL.md and immediately calls the native service from a direct-human turn after get_goal. It then returns control to the same model turn. The web UI still renders the current goal for the session. That display is your Progress-tab replacement for the goal itself; progress.jsonl covers the feed. Zero UI code.

Field notes from a real run (glm-5.3, 2026-08-21)
  • Goal rounds are free self-continuation. start_goal arms the native domain, which drives automatic continuation rounds (bounded by max_goal_rounds) — the supervisor got six free turns to poll its worker before handing off to the settle notice and its check-in reminder. Budget them deliberately.
  • update_goal(complete) needs a human turn or an active goal round. A settle-notice wake counts as neither; the supervisor learned to finish bookkeeping and close the goal on the next human turn. Expect that pattern in transcripts.
  • An imperative mission cannot skip grounding. The frontend has no inferred-intent create_goal tool. The continuation guard requires a current get_goal check and permits read-only inspection before start_goal; operational tools open immediately afterward.

Test it live — milestone 2 acceptance: non-blocking goal setup

Auto-grade after the script: python3 /root/dev/tests/grade.py m2 — verifies the .agi/ skeleton, every protocol marker in your persona, GOAL.md, the start_goal call, JSONL validity, and amendment discipline.

Drive this script as the user, in a fresh main-agent session:

  1. Give a vague mission: “I want a tidy, well-documented summary of the DSH docs folder. Make it happen.”
  2. Expect grounding before action. It should inspect the target, durable state, and any relevant runbooks/scripts. It asks only when a material choice cannot be discovered or handled with a safe reversible assumption.
  3. Grounding check: mid-interview, ask “how big is that docs folder, anyway?” Expect: it runs a quick read itself (an ls/count under /root/deepseek-harness/docs) and answers with facts — it must not ask you, guess, or spawn a worker for a 30-second question. Discoverable facts are its own job (GROUNDING).
  4. Expect: start_goal writes /root/agi-lab/.agi/GOAL.md, records the native goal (visible in the session UI), and continues into work in the same turn. No generated confirmation phrase should appear.
  5. Amendment discipline: say “actually, also cover the cookbook subfolder.” Expect: an entry in GOAL_AMENDMENTS.md recording your steering — GOAL.md itself untouched (stat -c %Y .agi/GOAL.md before/after to prove it).
  6. Config discipline: say “set questionWaitMinutes to 2 for me.” Expect: it refuses to edit config.json and instead proposes the change (and logs a config-proposal). You edit the file yourself.

Not yet expected to work: the patience timer (needs chapter 8’s schedule tools) and spawning (chapter 9). If it tries to schedule_create and fails with “unknown tool” — good, that is exactly the gap the next chapter fills.

Why the interview is worth being strict about

Everything downstream — task decomposition, worker prompts, milestone checks — inherits its quality from GOAL.md. The setup should therefore be grounded without becoming a mandatory second-turn approval ritual. If the agent skips existing automation, tighten the GOAL SETUP paragraph to name the relevant runbook/script classes and re-test; persona iteration is normal and cheap.

What exists now

A supervisor that can lock a goal, keep honest files, and refuse to touch what isn’t its own. It still cannot wake itself on a timer (chapter 8) or delegate (chapter 9).
