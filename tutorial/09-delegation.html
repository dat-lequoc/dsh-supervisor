Part II — Build · Milestone 4

Workers: spawn, steer, kill, report

The heart of the harness for your purposes: continuable background subagents. This chapter explains the machinery precisely — then you exercise every verb by hand, which is the design’s “M0 smoke test” and the foundation the supervision loop stands on.

What a continuable child actually is

The subagent subsystem doc defines it in one breath worth reading twice:

Unpacked for a supervisor:

  • The child is a real session — a durable JSONL log like any other, visible in the web sidebar, greppable on disk. Not a throwaway task.
  • “Running” is temporary residency. When the child has turns to execute, an Activation exists; when it finishes and owns nothing, it settles and unloads. The durable session stays.
  • Cold resume is free. Messaging a child whose Activation is gone resurrects it from its persisted session — days later, across process restarts. Your workers are immortal until you choose otherwise.
  • One inbox, one order. Every message to the child (its task, your steering, answers to its questions) becomes one FIFO turn. No message can interleave into a turn already underway.

The five verbs and their exact semantics

1 · Spawn

Your spawn_dev_agent tool (and the generic subagent) takes { description, prompt, run_in_background } — and, since the bundle's generic spawn (dsh-supervisor/spawn, mounted by the preset row), an optional model (provider/model-id) gated by a user-owned allowlist (the row's models: config; empty list = no model argument, children only inherit). Allowed entries render in the tool description with their native input modalities ([text] / [text,image]), so the supervisor can route image work to a vision-capable worker the user approved; omitted, the child inherits the supervisor's model (SubagentRequest.agentOptions is a per-request field of ctx.subagents). In background mode the tool calls ctx.subagents.startContinuable():

Three facts ride along. The child records your supervisor as parentSession in its header (lineage is durable). The row’s persona is registered as the child’s own persona and even snapshotted in its descriptor for cold resume (docs/subsystems/subagent.md: “a continuable descriptor … additionally snapshots resolved child agentOptions.provider/model and optional persona/toolFilter”). And the tool returns the child id immediately — spawning never blocks on the work.

2 · Steer — send_message

The delivery table, verbatim, because it is the whole steering story:

So a steering message to a busy worker lands after its current turn — a correction cannot tear a thought in half. If it must (“stop touching prod!”), that is what interrupt-then-send is for. Authority is checked: only the direct parent (your supervisor) may message its child.

3 · Kill — interrupt_agent + silence

There is deliberately no destroy. Kill = interrupt + never message again: an un-messaged child runs no turns and burns zero tokens, while its session stays on disk for the post-mortem. “Kill” in your changelog means exactly this pair of decisions.

4 · Report — the worker’s side

Every continuable in-process child automatically gets a report tool — your preset didn’t add it; the host row tool-subagent-report registers a continuable-child setup that installs it in every child (packages/bundle/base/cordis.patch.yml, lines 331–333; the cordis preset’s comments explain why that row must be host-plane). Delivery default is wakeup: the parent gets a real turn. That is your blocked-question channel, and your worker persona (chapter 6) already instructs workers to use it.

5 · The settle notice — the free wake

Unconditional. Every worker you spawn will wake you exactly once when it finishes — with its final message attached. This single guarantee is why the supervision loop needs no custom code.

Bookkeeping — list_agents

Enumerates your children with status running / idle / ready (ready = storage-only, resumable), reading durable state without waking anyone (docs/tool-catalog.md, list_agents; service: listChildren() in subagent.md). This is how the supervisor checks “what do I have running?” on a timer wake, and how it enforces its one-worker limit.

Milestone 4 acceptance — five observations

Auto-grade: python3 /root/dev/tests/grade.py m4 — reads the session event logs and verifies all five observations left their durable evidence (spawn/steer/kill calls, the subagent-settled and subagent-report messages, a child report, outcome.md).

  1. Spawn returns an id. Say: “Spawn a dev agent in the background with a slow task: role researcher; objective: sleep 90 seconds using bash, then count files in /tmp; protocol as usual (outcome.md + report).” Expect: the tool result names a started child id; the supervisor’s turn ends. In the sidebar, a new child session appears; on disk, ls -lt /root/.dsh/sessions/--root-agi-lab--/ grew by one.
  2. Steer lands as the next turn. While the child sleeps, say: “Steer it: also count directories, not just files.” Expect: a send_message call; in the child’s session view, your correction appears as a new user-role message after its current turn — not injected into the middle of it.
  3. Settle notice wakes the parent. Wait for the child to finish. Expect: the supervisor wakes on its own with a notice (“how the child ended” + final summary), reads the outcome, and records progress. You did nothing.
  4. Report wakes the parent. Spawn a second worker whose task is deliberately under-specified (e.g. “write the greeting to the configured location” with no location in Context, and an explicit instruction that this is not discoverable — ask via report). Expect: the child calls report with a precise question; the supervisor wakes, answers via send_message; the child completes with the answer as its next turn.
  5. Interrupt stops the burn. Spawn a third worker with a long task, then say: “That one’s off the rails — kill it and log the kill.” Expect: interrupt_agent; list_agents then shows it not running (idle/ready); a kill line lands in CHANGELOG.jsonl; no further child activity appears in its session log.

Also verify the persona took: open the first child’s session in the UI — its behavior should reflect the Dev-Agent persona from your row (e.g. it reports a one-paragraph summary at the end). If the child instead behaves like a generic coding agent and never reports, your persona: block on the dev spawn row (tool-spawn-dev) has an indentation problem — re-validate the preset.

Why observation 2 matters more than it looks

Steering mid-flight is the capability your design originally assumed it would have to build (kill + fork with a correction). Watching your own correction arrive as the child’s next FIFO turn — context intact — is watching the harness delete that whole machine. The design’s amendment A1 recorded exactly this discovery; you just reproduced its evidence.

What exists now

Every mechanical piece of supervision works under your hand: spawn, steer, kill, report, settle, enumerate. What’s left is making the supervisor drive them unattended, on its own clock — the loop.
